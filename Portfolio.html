Brain Tumor Detection using CNN

Jan 2026 – Apr 2026 · Co-developed with Yogarajan N

A full diagnostic pipeline that takes a raw MRI scan and returns a tumor type, an estimated size, a severity stage, and a set of personalized care recommendations — not just a classification label. Built around a custom CNN paired with fuzzy-logic segmentation to handle the blurry, gradient-heavy boundaries that make tumor margins hard to pin down with simple thresholding.

How it works

  1. Preprocessing. Scans are resized to 150×150 and pixel intensities normalized to 0–1, so the model isn't reacting to differences in brightness or contrast between scanners.
  2. Fuzzy segmentation. Instead of drawing a hard line between tumor and healthy tissue, a fuzzy-thresholding step assigns each pixel a membership value — a much better fit for MRI, where tumor edges blend gradually into surrounding tissue rather than stopping cleanly.
  3. Tumor sizing & staging. The segmented region is measured as a percentage of total brain area and automatically staged as small, medium, or large — giving a reproducible severity signal instead of a visual guess.
  4. CNN classification. A custom 4-block convolutional network (Conv2D → MaxPooling, with Batch Normalization and Dropout) classifies the scan as glioma, meningioma, pituitary tumor, or no tumor.
  5. Automated clinical report. The system generates a structured report — tumor type, size, stage — along with personalized diet, activity, and follow-up-frequency guidance tailored to the diagnosis.
  6. Deployment. Wrapped in a Gradio interface: upload a scan, get a full diagnostic dashboard back in real time, no ML background required to use it.

Why it holds up

Benchmarked against VGG16 and InceptionV3 — both strong general-purpose architectures that tend to overfit on smaller, specialized medical datasets — the custom CNN came out ahead on accuracy, inference speed, and its ability to pick up the subtle intensity-based cues that separate tumor types. The bigger differentiator, though, isn't the accuracy number: most baseline classifiers stop at a label, while this system turns that label into a full report a patient can actually act on.
